---
title: On Pre-College Math Education
date: 2026-08-05
category: thoughts
---

We teach students to compute long before we teach them to ask questions. By the time a student meets a real mathematical question, they've spent a decade learning that math means following procedures to get numbers that someone else already knows.

The best math classes I've seen invert this. The problem comes first — messy, concrete, and slightly wrong, the way real problems are. The technique arrives when it's needed, as a tool rather than a ritual. The answer matters least of all.

I don't think this requires genius teaching or rare talent. It requires patience: the willingness to let a classroom sit with confusion for a while, because that confusion is where the mathematics actually lives.

I'd like to write more about what such a class could look like in practice. This page is the start of that attempt.
